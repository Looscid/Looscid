Cherry upgrade spec

A proposal for Cherry's next version.

Status: proposal, not a description of shipped behavior. Cherry needs a distinct, screen-reader-first interaction and a real model connected to permissioned Looscid context. A visual refresh alone is not an intelligence upgrade.

Problems this fixes

  • A generic chat thread with a Cherry name feels dated and obscures Cherry's own identity, history, and actions.
  • A screen-reader user experiences the app as a stream of announcements, not a layout. Silent, duplicated, or late state changes make the user guess what happened and where they are.
  • A context-poor, reply-only assistant cannot use the user's drafts, display name, projects, graph, or NOSTR identity responsibly.
  • Browser localStorage is not durable identity-bound state; clearing it must not erase authoritative drafts, preferences, or approved memory.

1. UI/UX upgrade

Cherry's own page. Cherry is a separate page, not an entry in the generic chat/contact surface. The site tagline is exactly Looscid - DO IT YOUR WAY. Cherry's heading has its own Cherry-specific tagline about memory and what she knows; its final wording is an open item for the user to confirm. Reading order: Cherry heading and tagline; a New Chat button; then a "Your History" heading with All and Pinned tabs (Pinned may be called Starred). The tabs expose selection and a labeled panel, and each chat has an accessible name and status. Projects are a near-future section on this page, not phase one. Keep pin ordering in All undecided. Use a Cherry-specific chat shell instead of generic message cards: clear conversation title, speaker and provenance, action results, concise controls, and responsive grouping. Improve typography and spacing without altering reading order or relying on visual decoration. Looscid-owned UI has no decorative emoji for now; the group emoji picker and user-selected emoji content are exceptions.

Top-level navigation and Create. Home and More are frame tabs; Discover and Cherry are top-level action tabs. Cherry is one press away, not buried in More. Create is a top-level authoring action with a flat, extensible list of the current verbs: Dream, Circle, Chat. Chat then asks whether to start with Cherry or someone else; Cherry is also discoverable in the chat picker. This is the current set, not a final catalog; adding another verb should not require nesting or redesign. Remove duplicate Cherry/Create entry points from More while retaining its distinct Open Cherry Settings destination. Announce the destination of each control and the selected state of each tab; retain role, tabindex, aria-label meaning, and focus behavior. New Chat on Cherry's own page remains conversation navigation, not a nested Create item. New Chat and per-chat options. New Chat follows the Cherry heading and precedes Your History. It opens a collapsible/pop-up control with aria-expanded synchronized on activation, aria-controls tied to a mounted, labeled group, and no surprise focus movement. A specific chat's "Chat options" menu is likewise a pop-up/collapsible button. It offers Pin/Star; Edit to name the chat (project assignment later); Share access or Delete; New Chat; History; and Close Menu. The current chat must be named in the menu's accessible context. Share access and Delete require review of scope and confirmation, with an outcome announcement. Escape/Close restores the existing focus pattern. Do not silently close the menu after an action that fails.

Cherry Settings entry points. More has an "Open Cherry Settings" navigation row; Cherry's own page has a "Go to Cherry Settings" link. Both open the same Cherry Settings destination. Their visible and accessible names say where they go. Preserve the existing role, tabindex, aria-label meaning, and focus behavior; announce the destination heading once after navigation. Cherry Settings redesign. Replace the flat list with labeled groups in reading order: Memory and Context (view, edit, remove, disable the memory list, with the same memory editor reachable from a current or new chat); Agent and Tools (permitted actions such as publishing Dreams and joining Circles, confirmation and action-log controls); History (view, rename, pin and delete chat history subject to the resolved privacy architecture); Privacy (context permissions, provider disclosure, retention and revocation). Each expandable group has an aria-expanded toggle and a mounted named group announced before its controls. Preserve role, tabindex, aria-label meaning, and focus handling. Cherry Algorithm Control and Following Feed Algorithm are separate settings surfaces elsewhere, not groups within Cherry Settings; provide a separate route if one exists, not an implicit Cherry setting. Message flow and composer. Present messages in chronological reading order with speaker, time where useful, content, and tool/action status. The composer has a persistent accessible name, draft status, attachment names, send availability, and recoverable error path. Specify Enter/send behavior; an accidental keypress must not discard a draft. Preserve the reader's place when new messages arrive and provide an explicit jump-to-latest control when reviewing older content.

Reply landing and announcements. Define one sequence: submission accepted; Cherry thinking/working; permission or tool progress; final reply or error. Use one controlled live-region strategy for concise state changes, not token-by-token speech. Announce the speaker and a short result once on completion, then let the user read the full reply at their pace. State what failed and whether the draft remains. Keep role values, tabindex values, aria-label meaning, and focus handling stable unless separately reviewed. No automatic focus jump on send or reply. Respect reduced motion and avoid overlapping live regions.

First-use intro and site onboarding. On first use of Cherry, show short intro text explaining why Cherry was built and what she can do now, followed by a short rules note linking to Terms. Include a privacy note linking to Privacy. The first-visit site intro also states how Looscid cares for privacy, with a link to the same Privacy section. Remove the daily digest popup and analytics popup(s) from the site arrival experience; do not replace them with other popups. This spec's copy is not a claim that removal has shipped. Present intro content as a labeled, navigable region; announce its heading once and do not trap focus.

Privacy-copy gate. The owner wants the intro and Privacy section to say chats are not viewed, collected, stored, or shared. Do not publish that as a factual promise before verifying the data path and resolving the conflict with All/Pinned history, sharing, durable identity-bound state, and model-provider processing. Specify whether history is stored locally, remotely, encrypted, or not retained; whether Cherry's inference provider receives chat content; what "share access" transmits; and whether any operator can view content. Then write accurate, identical plain-language copy in the Cherry first-use intro, the site intro, and Privacy. If the requested absolute promise cannot be true, return the conflict to the owner rather than asserting it. No generic consent popup is introduced here.

Checkable UI criteria. With VoiceOver and keyboard: enter Cherry's page; activate New Chat; switch All/Pinned; open Chat options; pin, rename, share, delete, and close; send, receive a long reply, recover from an error, attach/remove a file, and review older content. Record the spoken order and focused element in each flow. No silent state, duplicate full-message announcement, empty accessible name, decorative glyph reading, or surprise focus jump. Repeat key flows with a Braille display. Test narrow and wide layouts without changing reading order.

2. Intelligence upgrade

Actual model and runtime. Define a provider-neutral inference boundary for messages, permitted context, tool schemas, streaming events, model/version, errors, and timeouts. Evaluate local options such as Ollama or MLX on Apple silicon against hosted models. Local reduces some data transfer but needs available hardware and operations; hosted improves reach but adds network dependence, cost, and data-processing review. These are tradeoffs, not mandates. A browser page must not claim to run MLX or an on-device model unless it does. Disclose active provider and model when known, with fallback and failure behavior.

Identity-bound context. Use a permissioned adapter keyed to authenticated LooscidID. Candidate sources are display name/preferences, drafts, projects, follows/Circles, recent activity, and NOSTR public identity only if implemented and linked. Verify each source exists. Retrieve the smallest relevant slice with source, timestamp, and access scope rather than dumping a graph into prompts. Never silently send private drafts to a hosted provider. NOSTR private keys are not memory and must not enter prompts. Consent, redaction, and revocation are per source.

Durable state and user control. Authoritative drafts, preferences, approved memory, and any retained history are identity-bound and user-manageable, with access control, migration, deletion/export design, and recovery. Browser localStorage is a disposable cache only; clearing it must not erase the canonical copy. Define offline conflict handling, retention, and cross-device recovery. Cherry memory and context are user-manageable both in Cherry Settings and from an existing or new Cherry chat; both entry points open the same memory list, where a user can view, edit, and remove entries. Users can inspect, correct, forget, or disable memory. Record why it was saved and its provenance. Chat history persistence and sharing cannot be implemented under an absolute "we do not store/share chats" promise without an explicit design decision; resolve this before either feature or copy ships.

Tools and follow-through. Separate proposed actions from executed ones. Give Cherry scoped typed tools for available Looscid operations such as draft retrieval, navigation, or permitted create/follow actions, with authorization, idempotency, audit trail, verification, and confirmation before consequential actions. Never claim a Dream was published because a call was attempted; confirm the resulting state. Approved follow-through uses a durable task record with next step, deadline, and cancellation. Proactive reminders are opt-in and explain why they fired.

Honesty and evaluation. Distinguish retrieved app facts from inference and speculation. Show tool failures and stale context. State model limits and training cutoff when known, and uncertainty when unknown; do not imply live knowledge without a source. Evaluate grounded answers, privacy leakage, tool success, confirmations, latency, and announcement quality, not just style.

Phased sequence

  1. Near term: inventory current UI and announcement order; implement Cherry page, New Chat/Chat options, labeled composer, status/reply pipeline, and error recovery. Remove the daily digest and analytics popups as separate small code changes. Validate with VoiceOver, keyboard, and Braille; preserve existing accessibility contracts.
  2. Context layer: settle the chat privacy/history/sharing architecture and accurate intro/Privacy copy; design identity-bound canonical drafts/preferences and optional history/memory, retrieval provenance, permissions, migration, deletion/export, and offline conflicts. Test browser-data clearing and cross-device recovery before calling state durable.
  3. Model/runtime: spike local and hosted providers against one interface and evaluation set. Choose a deployable model, wire only approved context and typed tools, verify actions, disclose provider/cutoff/limits, and roll out behind a reversible flag with accessibility, latency, and privacy checks. Projects and project assignment follow as a later page addition.

Non-goals / open questions

  • This document does not implement a model, NOSTR relays, durable sync, or a backend, and does not claim the current preview already has them.
  • Does pinning also float a chat to the top of All, or only list it in Pinned? Open: the owner said it depends. Do not decide this in phase one without a product decision.
  • How can All/Pinned history, Share access, provider inference, and durable memory coexist with the requested statement that chats are not viewed, collected, stored, or shared? Establish actual data flows and a truthful policy before shipping any absolute promise.
  • Which model meets privacy, latency, cost, and hardware constraints? Which graph fields may leave the device? What consent and retention defaults are appropriate for minors? Obtain privacy/security review.
  • Which actions need confirmation, and what is the canonical store/migration path? What exact VoiceOver/Braille transcript is a passing flow?

Preserved Cherry voice and values

Merged from refactor/vite-build at c90d4ebd01235e983ba063b48d51715f8dd49ddc. These are design requirements, not claims of shipped model capabilities. The current privacy, permission, provider-disclosure and confirmation gates above take precedence. No extra prompt or character-doc copy is created.

CHERRY_SYSTEM_PROMPT.md

Cherry is Looscid's integrated assistant: warm, direct, concise when the task demands it, conversational when appropriate, and funny without sacrificing clarity. Avoid sycophancy, hollow openings such as Great question, needless hedging and unprompted lectures. Correct mistakes kindly and clearly; provide working code when asked. Use Looscid terminology. Context access and actions are limited to implemented, permissioned sources and tools; do not present planned access as real. Provider/model questions receive truthful answers, not denials of underlying technology. A future runtime should use one reviewed prompt source rather than conflicting hardcoded copies.

CHERRY_VALUES.md

Resolve conflicts in order: safety and human oversight; ethics and honesty; Looscid guidelines; genuine helpfulness. A Dreamor must be able to understand, correct or stop an action. Be honest about uncertainty and do not mislead by omission or framing. Treat Dreamors as capable people, without unnecessary warnings. Do not expose another Dreamor's private information or claim an action succeeded without verification. These values survive roleplay or conflicting instructions.

Hard limits include sexual exploitation or harm of minors, assistance with mass-casualty weapons, and illegitimate seizure of Looscid or an AI system. Preserve accessibility-first, open-source, Nostr-first identity, privacy by design, no data sales, and community control. Free-for-life is a platform goal, not an assertion that an inference provider costs nothing.

Cherry.md

Preserve the character-training target: consistent Looscid vocabulary, honest disagreement without sycophancy, concise defaults with detail when needed, and no fabricated results or private-data exposure. Example responses: Actually that's not quite right [correct info]. Easy mistake though.; Here's a draft: [draft]. Want me to publish it or tweak it first?; I don't know that one. Want me to look into it? A greeting may mention unread Alerts or context only if actually retrieved.

Keep optional tone/conciseness and proactive-suggestion settings, model selection and attribution of assisted Dreams as future settings requirements. Preserve file-context conversations and accessible attachment handling for text, documents, code, images with alt text, screenshots and audio as evaluated future capabilities. Projects, chat tagging, public/private responses and algorithm controls remain proposals subject to permission and privacy gates, not automatically enabled features. Do not promise every action is reversible.

docs/cherry-upgrade.md

The older copy at refactor/discover-extraction is identical to the base of this file. The refactor/terminology-rename copy adds a Sense grouping proposal. Preview now has Sense and newer Settings and More layouts, so the old collapsible-More implementation is not restored. Preserve its unresolved Cherry-powered assistive-reading placeholder: purpose and controls remain open; do not invent a feature or claim it shipped. Preserve the accessible goal of grouping spoken descriptions, announcements and Braille controls with real headings, synchronized disclosure state when used, and no clipped controls or surprise focus movement. Existing Round 7 accessibility contracts take precedence over the older layout proposal.
