About Looscid

In short

  • Looscid is a free social app for everyone.
  • It works with every screen reader, and it's safe from flashing lights.
  • Something wrong? Tell me. I'll listen.
  • I want people with disabilities building it with me.

Why I'm building this

I'm Carter, and I'm blind.

I'm building Looscid because I believe apps should be for everyone.

If you have a problem, you should be heard. If you get banned, you should be heard.

That's why Looscid has a public Modlog. It's also why there's a Report Center, where you get a real reply.

YOU MATTER. PEOPLE MATTER.

I'm not building this to be nice. I'm building it because I care. Caring means doing the work.

What I promise you

Looscid is for everyone. No exceptions.

A lot of apps get built for one kind of person. Everyone else gets fixes later, if ever. I'm flipping that. Everyone comes first, from the first line of code.

Looscid is for you if you're:

  • blind or have low vision
  • Deaf or hard of hearing
  • living with a motor disability (it's hard to move parts of your body)
  • living with a cognitive or learning disability (thinking, memory, reading or focus)
  • living with seizures or a vestibular condition (your inner ear and balance)

Vision doesn't matter. Looscid is for everyone.

Here's what I promise:

  • Accessibility isn't an extra I add at the end. Everything else is built on top of it.
  • I build it with people who live with disabilities, not just for them.
  • I follow the rules for accessible apps. But they're where I start, not where I stop.
  • If an update breaks something for you, that's a bug. I fix it.
  • Every screen reader gets the same love, not just one.
  • If flashing lights or animations can hurt you, you still belong here. That's why there's Calm motion and flash blocking. Zero animation mode is coming too.

I don't do this to stay out of trouble. I do it because I won't let anyone get hurt or left out.

The research (with receipts)

Here's why this matters. Every fact has a source at the bottom of the page.

  • About 1.3 billion people have a significant disability. That's about 1 in 6 people in the world.
  • In February 2026, the WebAIM Million study checked the top one million home pages. 95.9% of them had accessibility failures a computer could find.
  • Those home pages had 56.1 accessibility errors each, on average.
  • That study only counted problems a computer can find. So the real number is probably higher.
  • Flashing lights can cause seizures in some people. Flashing 5 to 30 times a second is the most likely to do it.
  • WCAG (the main rules for accessible websites) says nothing should flash more than three times in one second. Looscid keeps to that limit.

Who Looscid is for

Each part below tells you four things: what the condition is, how it can mess with apps, a bit of history, and what Looscid does about it. Everyone's different, so the same condition can feel different for different people.

Blindness and low vision

What it is: Low vision is vision loss that glasses, contacts, medicine or surgery can't fix. It can make everyday stuff hard. Blindness is a lot of vision loss in both eyes.

At least 2.2 billion people in the world have a vision impairment.

How it can affect apps: Lots of blind people use a screen reader. A screen reader reads the screen out loud, or shows it in braille. Apps shut people out when pictures and buttons have no text. They also shut people out when text can't be made bigger.

History: Louis Braille began making his code of raised dots in 1824. He worked it out when he was 15. People read braille with their fingers.

What Looscid does: Looscid works with every screen reader and with braille displays. Vision settings change text size, contrast, bold text and spacing. Every screen has one main heading.

Deaf and hard of hearing

What it is: Hearing loss can be mild, moderate or severe. Deafness is a lot of hearing loss in both ears that can't be fixed.

Over 430 million people in the world need care for hearing loss that affects their daily life. By 2050, nearly 2.5 billion people may have some hearing loss.

How it can affect apps: Videos and audio need captions or a transcript. A transcript is the words written out. For some Deaf people, sign language is their first language. Simple written words help them too.

History: In the 1700s, Charles-Michel de l'Épée started a school for deaf people in Paris. He paid for it himself.

What Looscid does: Hearing settings turn on captions for audio. They can also show you a visual cue instead of a sound.

Photosensitive epilepsy (flashing and strobe)

What it is: Epilepsy can cause seizures. For some people with epilepsy, flashing lights or some patterns can trigger a seizure. That's called photosensitive epilepsy.

About 1 in 100 people has epilepsy. About 3% of them have photosensitive epilepsy.

How it can affect apps: Flashing on a screen can cause seizures, migraines, dizziness and nausea. A strobe is a light that flashes fast. Flashing 5 to 30 times a second is the most likely to trigger a seizure. It's different for each person.

History: On December 16, 1997, many children in Japan had seizures while watching a cartoon on TV. Doctors studied what happened.

What Looscid does: Block flashing and strobes is on from the start. Nothing in Looscid flashes more than three times a second. Calm motion starts out matching your device's Reduce Motion setting, and you can flip it yourself. Coming next: flash blocking will also catch strobe effects in Dreams, and Zero animation mode will stop all motion with one switch.

Vestibular disorders (motion sensitivity)

What it is: The vestibular system is parts of your inner ear and brain. It helps control your balance and your eye movements. If it's hurt or sick, it can cause dizziness and balance problems.

One big study looked at adults in the United States aged 40 or older. Up to 35% of them have had a vestibular problem. That's about 69 million people.

How it can affect apps: Motion on a screen can cause dizziness, nausea and headaches for some people. One example is parallax scrolling. That's when the background moves at a different speed than the page.

History: In 1861, Prosper Ménière described patients with spells of vertigo and hearing loss. Vertigo is a spinning feeling. Back then, doctors thought vertigo came from the brain. Ménière pointed to the inner ear.

What Looscid does: Calm motion stops things from sliding and moving. It follows your device's setting until you flip it. Zero animation mode is coming.

Motor and mobility disabilities

What it is: A motor disability can mean weak muscles or less control over them. Examples are tremors (shaking), trouble with coordination, and paralysis.

How it can affect apps: Lots of people use special keyboards, switches, mouth sticks or head pointers instead of a mouse. Apps block them when something doesn't work with a keyboard. Apps also block them when time limits are too short. Small buttons are hard to press with a tremor.

History: In 1861, a surgeon named William John Little linked cerebral palsy to hard births. Cerebral palsy affects body movement and muscle control. In 1897, Sigmund Freud said most cases start before birth.

What Looscid does: Motor settings make buttons bigger. They add a strong outline that shows where you are. Looscid is built to work with a keyboard and with switches.

Cognitive and learning disabilities

What it is: These can affect learning, reading, memory, attention and understanding new things. Two examples are ADHD and dyslexia.

ADHD can make it hard to pay attention, stay on task or stay organized. It can also bring hyperactivity (lots of energy that's hard to calm) and acting before thinking. Dyslexia makes reading hard. It's mostly trouble matching speech sounds to letters and words.

How it can affect apps: Apps get hard to use when they have long forms or confusing menus. Long, hard sentences make it worse. So does moving or flickering stuff you can't turn off.

History: In 1896, Dr. W. Pringle Morgan wrote about Percy. Percy was a bright boy who had a hard time learning to read. He was one of the first children described with what's now called dyslexia. In 1902, Dr. George Still described 43 children with serious attention problems. That's seen as an early description of ADHD.

What Looscid does: This page is written in plain words. Vision settings have dyslexia-friendly spacing. Calm motion cuts down on movement. Zero animation mode is coming.

  • Turn on high contrast and bold text.
  • Turn on dyslexia-friendly spacing.

  • Turn on captions for audio.
  • Get a visual cue instead of a sound.

Open Hearing settings

Motor

For you if small buttons are hard to press, or you use a keyboard or a switch.

  • Make buttons bigger.
  • Turn on a strong outline that shows where you are.

Open Motor settings

Motion

For you if movement or flashing can hurt you. It's one switch for motion, plus flash blocking that stays on.

  • Calm motion is one switch for all of it: no sliding or moving, softer sounds and gentler vibration. It starts out matching your device's Reduce Motion setting. Flip it yourself and your choice sticks. If your device already asks for less motion, Looscid does it for you.
  • Block flashing and strobes is on from the start, and turning Calm motion off never turns it off. Nothing in Looscid flashes more than three times a second. That limit is always on.

Works with every screen reader

Some apps only get tested with one screen reader. I build Looscid for all of them. That means VoiceOver, TalkBack, NVDA, JAWS, Orca and braille displays, like the BrailleNote and the Monarch.

What every screen gets

  • One main heading, so you can always jump to the top of a page.
  • Pictures with alt text (words that say what's in the picture).
  • A label on every text box.
  • Links that say where they go.
  • Buttons with real names. "Delete Dream", not just "Delete". And never "Save button", because your screen reader already says it's a button.
  • Errors that get read out to you.
  • Color is never the only clue. There are always words too.
  • Focus (the spot your screen reader or keyboard is on) only moves when you do something. Nothing yanks you around.

Menus, sheets and pop-ups

When a menu, sheet or pop-up opens, everything behind it is hidden from your screen reader, braille and keyboard. So you can't get lost behind it.

Escape closes it. On iPhone, a two-finger scrub (draw a quick Z with two fingers) closes it too. When it closes, you land back on the button that opened it.

Keyboard

Looscid is built so you can use it with just a keyboard.

  • Tab moves between things.
  • In menus and tabs, the Arrow keys move, and Home and End jump to the first and last one.
  • Escape closes menus and pop-ups.
  • Enter sends a message from a text box.

Braille

Looscid is built for braille displays too. The Enter key on your braille display sends, the same as a keyboard. You can also pick how buttons show up on braille in Sense settings.

The rules I follow

WCAG is a set of rules for making websites accessible. ADA is a United States law about disability rights.

Looscid aims to follow WCAG 2.2 and the spirit of the ADA. It hasn't been checked by an outside expert yet. If something doesn't work for you, tell me. I'll fix it.

How I test it (and how you can too)

I want real people with disabilities testing Looscid. Here's how I test, and you can try it too:

  1. Use it with your screen reader.
  2. Put the mouse away and use only the keyboard.
  3. Zoom in to 200%.
  4. Turn on high contrast and see if you can still read everything.
  5. Turn on Calm motion and check that nothing slides around.

If anything trips you up, that's a bug. Tell me about it (here's how, down below).

What's not done yet

I'd rather be honest than act like it's perfect lol. Here's what isn't ready yet:

  • Zero animation mode, one switch that stops all motion. It's coming.
  • Flash blocking catching strobe effects in Dreams. Coming next.
  • An appeal button for bans. For now, use Send Feedback.
  • A check by an outside accessibility expert. Hasn't happened yet.

Got a problem? Tell me

If something's wrong, you should be heard. And you should get a real answer.

The Report Center

The Report Center is one place for every report. You can report:

  • a Dream
  • a person
  • a Circle
  • a bug or an idea
  • a child safety problem

Reports about Dreams, people, Circles and child safety are private. Only you and the Looscid mods see them. Mods are the people who keep Looscid safe.

My reports shows every report you made, and what happened to each one.

Found an accessibility bug?

Report it as a bug in the Report Center, or use Send Feedback. It helps a ton if you tell me:

  • your screen reader, or other tool, and your device
  • what you did
  • what you heard or saw, and what you expected

Send Feedback opens a public issue on GitHub, so don't put anything private in it.

The Modlog

The Modlog is public. It lists every Dream the mods remove, with the date and the reason. A Dream can't be removed without a reason. You'll find it in the Help Center, under Modlog.

If you get banned

If you get banned, you should be heard too. Same if something of yours gets removed and you think it's wrong. A ban isn't a gag order. You get to make your case, and I'll listen.

There's no appeal button yet. For now, tell me with Send Feedback. Send Feedback opens a public issue on GitHub. Anyone can read it. So don't put anything private in it.

What makes Looscid different

Open source

Open source means anyone can read the code. Looscid uses the GNU AGPL-3.0 license. You can check what it does and help build it.

Likes and Not interested

Like what you like. The Liked tab keeps every like in one place. Not into something? Pick Not interested in the Dream's More menu and you'll see less like it. Only you ever see that, and Looscid never shows a count for it.

Dreams and Circles

A Dream is what you share on Looscid. A Circle is a group of people around one thing you like. People on Looscid are called Dreamors.

No ads

Looscid has no ads. It doesn't sell your data.

Got an idea?

I want to hear it, big or small.

Some of the best ideas on the roadmap came from people in the epilepsy community. Zero animation mode came from them. So did strobe checks in flash blocking. 💜
