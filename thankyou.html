Thank You / Acknowledgements

Looscid is built with gratitude for the open source projects and people who make a more open, accessible, and human internet possible.

noStrudel by hzrd149

What it is

noStrudel is an accessible web client for exploring the Nostr protocol.

How it helped Looscid

noStrudel was especially important to Looscid. Its relay pool management, event signing and handling architecture, and client-side local-first approach helped shape the way Looscid works with Nostr while keeping the experience responsive and user-centered.

Our sincere thanks to hzrd149 for building and sharing noStrudel. Its ideas and implementation were an important foundation for Looscid, and we are grateful for the care it represents.

https://github.com/hzrd149/nostrudel

Foundational Projects

nostr-tools (nbd-wtf)

Thank you to nbd-wtf for nostr-tools, the core JavaScript library that makes reliable Nostr integration possible. Its event creation, signing, relay connections, and NIP implementations provided essential building blocks for Looscid.

https://github.com/nbd-wtf/nostr-tools

Nostr protocol

Thank you to the Nostr protocol community for creating an open, decentralized foundation for identity and social connection. The protocol gives Looscid a way to build without placing users under the control of a single company.

https://github.com/nostr-protocol/nostr

Nostr Nests

Thank you to the Nostr Nests team for pioneering decentralized audio rooms and real-time social experiences. Their work helped inform Looscid's approach to calls, live audio, and shared presence.

https://github.com/nostrnests/nests

Yapper & Mysty Social (MysteryNBS)

Thank you to MysteryNBS for sharing Yapper and Mysty Social. Their open approach to building social applications offered thoughtful references for Looscid's structure, interaction patterns, and belief in accessible community software.

https://github.com/MysteryNBS/mysty-social-public

VoltAgent

Thank you to the VoltAgent team for open source agent architecture and practical design patterns. Their work helped shape the ideas behind Cherry and Looscid's approach to helpful, capable software agents.
