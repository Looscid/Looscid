# About Looscid

## In short

- Looscid is a free social app for everyone.
- It works with every screen reader, and it's safe from flashing lights.
- I want to hear you if something goes wrong.
- I want people with disabilities to help build it.

## Why I'm building this — Carter

I'm Carter, and I'm blind.

I'm building Looscid because I believe apps should be for everyone.

If you have a problem, you should be heard. If you get banned, you should be heard.

That's why Looscid has a public mod log. It's also why there's a Report Center, where you get a real reply.

YOU MATTER. PEOPLE MATTER.

I'm not building this to be nice. I'm building it because I care. Caring means doing the work.

## My commitment

Looscid is for everyone. No exceptions.

Many apps are built for one kind of person. Other people get fixes later, if ever. I'm doing it the other way around. Everyone comes first, from the first line of code.

Looscid is for you if you are:

- blind or have low vision
- Deaf or hard of hearing
- living with a motor disability (it's hard to move parts of your body)
- living with a cognitive or learning disability (thinking, memory, reading or focus)
- living with seizures or a vestibular condition (your inner ear and balance)

Vision does not matter. Looscid is for everyone.

I build for every screen reader, not just one. That means VoiceOver, TalkBack, NVDA, JAWS, Orca, and more.

Flashing lights or animations can hurt some people. You still belong here. That's why Looscid has Flash safety and Calm mode. Zero animation mode is coming too.

I don't do this to stay out of trouble. I do it because I won't let anyone get hurt or left out.

## The research

Here are some facts about why this matters.

- About 1.3 billion people have a significant disability. That's about 1 in 6 people in the world.
- In February 2026, the WebAIM Million study checked the top one million home pages. 95.9% of them had accessibility failures a computer could find.
- Those home pages had 56.1 accessibility errors each, on average.
- The study only counted problems a computer can find. So the real number is likely higher.
- Flashing lights can cause seizures in some people. Flashing 5 to 30 times a second is the most likely to do it.
- WCAG says nothing should flash more than three times in one second. Looscid keeps to that limit.

The sources are listed at the end of this page.

## The conditions Looscid is built for

Each part below says four things. What the condition is. How it can affect apps. A bit of history. And what Looscid does about it. Everyone is different, so a condition can affect people in different ways.

### Blindness and low vision

What it is: Low vision is vision loss that glasses, contacts, medicine or surgery can't fix. It can make everyday tasks hard. Blindness is a lot of vision loss in both eyes.

At least 2.2 billion people in the world have a vision impairment.

How it can affect apps: Many blind people use a screen reader. A screen reader reads the screen out loud, or shows it in braille. Apps can shut people out when pictures and buttons have no text. Apps can also shut people out when text can't be made bigger.

History: Louis Braille began making his code of raised dots in 1824. He worked it out when he was 15. People read braille with their fingers.

What Looscid does: Looscid works with every screen reader and with braille displays. Vision settings change text size, contrast, bold text and spacing. Every screen has one main heading.

### Deaf and hard of hearing

What it is: Hearing loss can be mild, moderate or severe. Deafness is a lot of hearing loss in both ears that can't be fixed.

Over 430 million people in the world need care for hearing loss that affects their daily life. By 2050, nearly 2.5 billion people may have some hearing loss.

How it can affect apps: Videos and audio need captions or a transcript. A transcript is the words written out. For some Deaf people, sign language is their first language. Simple written words help them too.

History: In the 1700s, Charles-Michel de l'Épée started a school for deaf people in Paris. He paid for it himself.

What Looscid does: Hearing settings turn on captions for audio. They can also show a visual cue instead of a sound.

### Photosensitive epilepsy (flashing and strobe)

What it is: Epilepsy can cause seizures. For some people with epilepsy, flashing lights or some patterns can trigger a seizure. This is called photosensitive epilepsy.

About 1 in 100 people has epilepsy. About 3% of them have photosensitive epilepsy.

How it can affect apps: Flashing on a screen can cause seizures, migraines, dizziness and nausea. A strobe is a light that flashes fast. Flashing 5 to 30 times a second is the most likely to trigger a seizure. This is different for each person.

History: On December 16, 1997, many children in Japan had seizures while watching a cartoon on TV. Doctors studied what happened.

What Looscid does: Flash safety is on from the start. Nothing in Looscid flashes more than three times a second. Calm mode is on from the start too. Coming next: Flash safety will also catch strobe effects. Zero animation mode will stop all motion with one switch.

### Vestibular disorders (motion sensitivity)

What it is: The vestibular system is parts of your inner ear and brain. It helps control your balance and your eye movements. If it is hurt or sick, it can cause dizziness and balance problems.

One large study looked at adults in the United States aged 40 or older. Up to 35% of them have had a vestibular problem. That's about 69 million people.

How it can affect apps: Motion on a screen can cause dizziness, nausea and headaches for some people. One example is parallax scrolling. That's when the background moves at a different speed than the page.

History: In 1861, Prosper Ménière described patients with spells of vertigo and hearing loss. Vertigo is a spinning feeling. Back then, doctors thought vertigo came from the brain. Ménière pointed to the inner ear.

What Looscid does: Reduce Motion stops things from sliding and moving. It follows your device's setting. Calm mode means less movement. Zero animation mode is coming.

### Motor and mobility disabilities

What it is: A motor disability can mean weak muscles or less control over them. Examples are tremors (shaking), trouble with coordination, and paralysis.

How it can affect apps: Many people use special keyboards, switches, mouth sticks or head pointers instead of a mouse. Apps can block them when something doesn't work with a keyboard. Apps can also block them when time limits are too short. Small buttons are hard to press with a tremor.

History: In 1861, a surgeon named William John Little linked cerebral palsy to hard births. Cerebral palsy affects body movement and muscle control. In 1897, Sigmund Freud said most cases start before birth.

What Looscid does: Motor settings make buttons larger. They add a strong outline that shows where you are. Looscid is built to work with a keyboard and with switches.

### Cognitive and learning disabilities

What it is: These can affect learning, reading, memory, attention and understanding new information. Two examples are ADHD and dyslexia.

ADHD can make it hard to pay attention, stay on task or stay organized. It can also bring hyperactivity (lots of energy that's hard to calm) and acting before thinking. Dyslexia makes reading hard. It's mostly trouble matching speech sounds to letters and words.

How it can affect apps: Apps can be hard to use when they have long forms or confusing menus. Long, hard sentences make it worse. So does moving or flickering content you can't turn off.

History: In 1896, Dr. W. Pringle Morgan wrote about Percy. Percy was a bright boy who had a hard time learning to read. He was one of the first children described with what is now called dyslexia. In 1902, Dr. George Still described 43 children with serious attention problems. This is seen as an early description of ADHD.

What Looscid does: This page is written in plain words. Vision settings have dyslexia-friendly spacing. Calm mode cuts down on movement. Zero animation mode is coming.

## Accessibility settings

You find all of these in Looscid. Go to Settings, then Accessibility.

### Vision

Vision helps if you are blind, have low vision, or want things easier to see.

- Change the text size.
- Turn on high contrast and bold text.
- Turn on dyslexia-friendly spacing.

### Hearing

Hearing helps if you are Deaf or hard of hearing.

- Turn on captions for audio.
- Get a visual cue instead of a sound.

### Motor

Motor helps if small buttons are hard to press. It also helps if you use a keyboard or a switch.

- Make buttons larger.
- Turn on a strong outline that shows where you are.

### Motion

Motion helps if movement or flashing can hurt you. It has three switches.

- Flash safety is on from the start. Nothing in Looscid flashes more than three times a second. That limit is always on.
- Calm mode is on from the start too. It means less movement, softer sounds and gentler vibration.
- Reduce Motion stops things from sliding and moving. If your device already asks for less motion, Looscid does it for you.

### Sense

Sense has the screen reader settings. It covers speech, how much is said, and braille.

### Commandbar

Commandbar settings are in Settings, then General. They change how Commandbar answers are shown and read. For example, each command can get its own heading. Then a screen reader can jump between them.

## Every screen reader

Some apps get tested with only one screen reader. I build Looscid for all of them. That includes VoiceOver, TalkBack, NVDA, JAWS, Orca and braille displays.

Every screen has one main heading. So you can always jump to the top of a page.

## Accessibility standards

WCAG is a set of rules for making websites accessible. ADA is a United States law about disability rights.

Looscid aims to follow WCAG 2.2 and the spirit of the ADA. It hasn't been checked by an outside expert yet. If something doesn't work for you, tell me. I'll fix it.

I want real people with disabilities to test Looscid.

## Report a problem or appeal a ban

If something is wrong, you should be heard. You should get a real answer.

### The Report Center

The [Report Center](https://looscid.github.io/Looscid/Report/) is one place for every report. You can report:

- a Dream
- a person
- a Circle
- a bug or an idea
- a child safety problem

Reports about Dreams, people, Circles and child safety are private. Only you and the Looscid mods see them. Mods are the people who keep Looscid safe.

My reports shows every report you made. It also shows what happened to each one.

### The mod log

The mod log is public. It lists every Dream the mods remove. It shows the date and the reason. A Dream can't be removed without a reason. You can find it in the Help Center, under Mod log.

### If you get banned

If you get banned, you should be heard too. The same goes if something of yours is removed and you think it's wrong.

There's no appeal button yet. For now, tell me with Send Feedback. Send Feedback opens a public issue on GitHub. Anyone can read it. So don't put anything private in it.

## What makes Looscid different

### Open source

Open source means anyone can read the code. Looscid uses the GNU AGPL-3.0 license. You can check what it does and help build it.

### Likes and dislikes

Like what you like. Dislike what you don't. Only you can see your dislikes. Looscid never shows a dislike count. The Liked tab keeps both in one place.

### Dreams and Circles

A Dream is what you share on Looscid. A Circle is a group of people around one thing you like. People on Looscid are called Dreamors.

### No ads

Looscid has no ads. It doesn't sell your data.
